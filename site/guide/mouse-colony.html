Your databases

Mouse colony

Mice, cages, litters and breeders, from birth to weaning: racks laid out like yours, and genotyping and weaning dates worked out for you.

What this does for your week

A colony runs on dates that are easy to get wrong and tedious to keep: when a litter is old enough to genotype, when it must be weaned, which cage is getting crowded, whose mice they are. BioManager's job is to work those out from one thing you already know — the day the pups were born — and then put them in front of whoever is on duty.

Pups are bornYou record the date once (Record a litter). Genotyping and weaning dates are worked out from it and appear on Home and the calendar — the same list in both, so nobody keeps a private one.
They are old enough to genotypeThe litter becomes one record per pup in a few clicks (Genotype a litter), each with its own ear tag and result, instead of a row you retype.
Weaning dayWean a litter moves the pups into new cages by sex, keeps them attached to the litter they came from, and frees the breeding cage. The cage cards for the new cages print with the genotypes already on them (Print cage cards).
In the animal roomScan the QR code on a cage card with a phone to open that cage, add a weight or a note with gloves on, and have it in the record before you leave the room (At the bench).

What you stop doing: keeping the wean dates in your head or a wall calendar, retyping a litter into a sheet, rewriting cage cards by hand after a wean, and asking whose mouse a cage holds — every record carries its owner, and who may change it.

Tabs across the top: Mice, Cages, Litters, Breeders, Experiments, Strains and Dropdowns. The My colony / My groups / Shared / Everyone switch changes what you see, never what you may edit: My colony is yours plus the shared cages you work in, My groups your project groups', Shared just the shared cages, Everyone every cage. Above the cages, All, Active (cages with living mice) and a button for each purpose show only those cages.

Racks and cages

Cages → Rack grid → New rack: rows, columns and how positions are labelled — D7, 4-7, 7D or 1 to 80, like the stickers on your real racks. Cages without a position wait in Unplaced; drag them on. Each cage has a purpose, an owner, a room and its mice. The purposes:

  • Breeding: a mating cage, mating now. Only a Breeding cage has Litter born, Genotyping and Wean.
  • Breeder: the lab's breeding stock, the mice kept to be bred. Each person keeps part of the lab's stock, so a Breeder cage is shared with the lab unless its owner makes it personal, and the Breeders tab lists these cages.
  • Experiment: mice in an experiment.

An admin changes the list under Configure → Cage purposes (or the Dropdowns tab, the same list), and each purpose added there gets its button above the cages. A cage counts as Active while it holds living mice. Select cages to set purpose or owner, move them, or Retire the empty ones: each leaves its rack and loses its purpose.

The cages show three ways. Table: one row per cage, each with its mice beneath it (edit them in place) and its actions; Litter born and Wean due come right after the mice, so when to wean is in view. Type over a cage's number to renumber it (its mice go with it; a number another cage has is refused). Card ID, right after the number, is the number on the facility's own cage card, if it has one. A cage's mice show the transgene columns the Mice tab shows: show or hide Transgene 3 or 4 there under Columns, and the cages follow. Close all folds every cage to its row (the page remembers), and Open all opens them again; the arrow at the start of a row or its mice count opens or closes one. Cards: a card for each cage — where it is, its purpose, owner, strain and when to wean, its mice and the same actions — with its own search and the same buttons, starting on Active. Rack grid: the racks from the front. The page remembers the one you used last.

Mice

  • New mouse for one, Add many for a new group (or a CSV made from its Download a template), and Import from Excel for the sheet you already keep (how). In Add many, type new as the cage to put a group in one new cage; with females and males, each sex gets its own new cage unless you tick Keep both sexes in one new cage. In the preview, new-2, new-3… are further new cages, and a new cage that would hold both sexes is pointed out.
  • Each mouse has a Custom tag, sex, date of birth (age shown in weeks), status — breeder, experiment, geno, transfer, sac — up to four transgenes, a genotype, cage, litter, owner and notes. A date of birth can't be in the future.
  • The number BioManager gives a mouse is its identity: handed out in order, never reused, and what its samples, cage card and experiments all link by. Custom tag, beside it, is however the animal itself is marked — an ear tag number, an ear punch such as RF or LB, a tail tattoo, or your lab's own way. Two mice may carry the same mark, search finds a mouse by it, and a spreadsheet column called custom tag, ear tag, notch, tattoo or tail tattoo comes into it when you import. Point at a mouse's number and its tag shows there too. If your lab doesn't mark its mice, untick Custom tag under Columns to hide it.
  • Select mice to set a field (owner, status, transgenes, cage, note, date of death), Add to experiment, or Sac. Setting sac or a date of death ends the record; it stays in the history.

Breeding: from litter to weaning

Set up a mating by putting the breeders in a cage whose purpose is Breeding. On the Cages tab, open it (its arrow or mice count) or find its card under Cards: Litter born, Genotyping and Wean are there, and only on Breeding cages. Create litter numbers each litter L-1, L-2, L-3… (type your own ID instead if you like).

  1. Litter born asks for the date of birth (today unless you change it; a date in the future is refused) and records the birth. BioManager works out weaning (P21) and genotyping (P28, or the day set in Settings → General), which appear on Home and the calendar when due — the same list in both. A cage holds one litter date, so a new litter replaces the last: if those pups are still in the cage, wean them first.
  2. Genotyping asks for the mouse numbers of the father and mother, and how many pups were born; Create litter makes the litter and a mouse for each pup, set to geno. They wait in the genotyping queue on Home until you change their Status from geno; entering a genotype alone doesn't take them off it.
  3. Wean opens Wean and distribute pups, already filled with the cage's pups, females and males on separate rows: Wean and move sends each row to a new or existing cage, or Mark weaned, move pups later. Either way the litter is marked weaned and leaves Home and the calendar. Pups younger than P18 are weaned only after you confirm.

Breeders lists the Breeder cages, the lab's breeding stock: rack and position, and how many of each sex. Above them, the breeders in Breeder and Breeding cages are counted by genotype, and breeders past 30 weeks are flagged. Pick takes a breeder as yours to set up a mating; its owner is notified.

Experiments, strains, dropdowns

  • Experiments group mice into a cohort with treatment groups, what is done to them day by day and their body weights: see Experiments, for every animal. (The notebook's experiments are the write-ups; these are the animals.)
  • Strains is the lab's reference list of lines and alleles.
  • Dropdowns keeps the choices each column offers, so the lab uses the same words. Everyone sees them; only admins change them.

Data and help

Your data and backups

Where your data is kept, how it's backed up, and how to move it.

The desktop app

macOS~/Library/Application Support/Biomanager/
Windows%APPDATA%\Biomanager\
Linux~/.local/share/Biomanager/
  • Your data lives outside the app, so updating or reinstalling never touches it.
  • Before a new version changes the database an older one made, it copies it to data/backups/before-upgrade-….db in that folder, next to the database itself, data/biomanager.db (the last ten are kept). Every release is tested opening a lab made by each earlier release, with nothing lost.
  • Back that folder up with Time Machine or File History, copying it while BioManager is closed.
Don't keep BioManager's data in Dropbox, OneDrive, Google Drive or iCloud Drive. Syncing corrupts database files. BioManager warns you if it finds itself in one.

The whole lab in one file

Settings → Your data → Save the whole lab (admins) puts every record, account and uploaded file in one .biomanager file: in Downloads on the desktop app, downloaded from a server. It is a backup you can carry, and the way to bring the lab to another computer (Moving to a new computer? Bring a lab file here on a new desktop app's first page) or to a new server (Bring a lab from the desktop app). Stored connections such as Google Calendar links are left out: they are made again where the lab goes.

Labs set aside

Start a new lab (Settings → Lab, or the sign-in when nobody can sign in) sets the lab aside, whole, and opens an empty one. Bring back a lab set aside (Settings → Lab, the sign-in, or a new lab's first page) lists them with their names and accounts; the lab there now is set aside in its place, so either step can be undone the same way. Nothing is deleted.

A lab server

A server backs up every night, restores the newest backup every week to prove it works, and can keep encrypted copies off-site — see Keeping the data safe.

The lab's key

Every lab has its own key. The server's nightly backups, Save the whole lab files and the copies on your computers are locked with it, so anyone who gets hold of one (a lost laptop, a backup disk, a file sent to the wrong person) sees only random bytes. The server, and the desktop apps of the lab's admins that join it, hold the key and open these files for you; you never type it in day to day.

  • Settings → Your data → Lab key (admins) shows the key and when its recovery key was last shown, and to whom. Show the recovery key asks for your password, then shows it. Write it down or keep it in a password manager, away from the backups.
  • If the server and every admin's computer were lost, the recovery key is the only way to open a backup. A new server restores with it (Restore a backup), and a lab file or copy opens with it under A lab back without its key on the same page, or when you bring the lab file in.
  • Anyone with the recovery key can read the lab's backups: keep it like the server's own password.

A copy of the lab on your computer

When the desktop app joins your lab (Open your lab, below), it keeps a copy of the lab on that computer by itself, so the lab's records live on more than one machine: a fresh one every day while the app is open, checked, the last 14 kept, with the uploaded files mirrored. Settings → Devices & copies on the computer shows when the last copy was made.

A computer that shouldn't join can still keep a copy with a key: on the server, Settings → Devices & copies, name the computer and Make a key (shown once); in that computer's desktop app, enter the server's address and the key, and Save and copy now. (Admins always may; members when Settings → People & access → What members may do allows it.) Revoke stops a computer from taking more.

Each copy is locked with the lab's key and with that computer's own, so it opens on that computer and nowhere else without the recovery key. An admin's copy is the whole lab, and can rebuild the server if it's ever lost (how): treat a computer holding one like the server itself. A member's copy holds what they can see in the app: no one's password, not other people's private notebook pages, personal databases or personal to-dos and events, not the Audit log. Guests can't keep a copy.

One lab on many devices

One device holds the lab's master copy; every other one works on it through its address, so nothing is ever merged and nothing written in two places is lost. Settings → Devices & copies → Devices shows which device it is, and every computer linked to the lab: how it uses the lab, its version, when it was last heard from.

Your devices

Every computer, phone and browser you are signed in on is one of your devices, listed under Your devices in Settings → Devices & copies: what it is, when it was last seen and from where. Rename one by typing its new name; Sign out ends it there at once, without changing your password (a lost phone, a shared computer). Admins also see Everyone's devices.

  • The desktop app joins the lab as one of your devices. Open your lab (on a new desktop app's first page, or Go → Your Lab's BioManager) finds the lab's address, then asks you to sign in as you do at the lab. Signed in with Google or Microsoft? Use a code: it shows a code to type under A device shows a code? on a computer or phone already signed in. The app's window then works on the lab through the app itself, never the server's page, and keeps its daily copy. Go → This Computer's BioManager comes back to its own lab, and Go → Your Lab's BioManager returns to the lab. Sign out in its window asks Sign this computer out of the lab?, which ends the device and forgets what it kept of the lab.
  • The phone apps become one of your devices once you sign in through them, which lets them keep the facility on the phone for working without a signal (Working offline).
  • A browser you sign in on is listed too; signing it out from another device signs it out there.
  • A lab server is the master copy unless an admin hands it on.
  • Open the lab in this window (a linked desktop): the app joins the lab as one of your devices, as above.
  • Share this lab on the network (a desktop, admins): the computer becomes the master for every device on the same network, which opens the address it shows and signs in with an account there. It must stay on and awake. The connection isn't encrypted, so share only on a network you trust; for anywhere else, a lab server. Its own pages (setting up a server, its copies, its devices) are never offered to another device.
  • Make it the master (on the master, admins): for a linked desktop with an admin's key, open now, on the same version. Within a minute it freezes the master (read only, so no change is lost), takes a last copy, keeps its own data in backups/before-taking-over-….db, and shares the lab on its network. The old master stays read only, with a banner pointing everyone there. Everyone signs in again with their lab account. Sign-ins to other services (Google Calendar) are not carried and are connected again.
  • Give the master copy back (on the desktop holding it): its database and new files go back; the other device keeps what it had in backups/before-taking-back-….db and is the master again. The desktop turns read only and opens the lab there; Go back to this computer's own lab restores what it had before it took over.
  • If the computer holding the master copy is lost, an admin on the old master chooses Make this the master again: what was changed on the lost computer since is not there.

Getting your data out

Export on any sheet downloads what it shows as CSV, which opens in Excel. On the Mice sheet, Export → Excel is a workbook that Import from Excel reads back. A note that starts with = stays text in every export, never a formula. Export my data (in Settings → Your data) downloads your own mice, cages, weights, experiments and plasmids as CSV, each plasmid's sequence as a GenBank file with its features and the files kept with it, your notebook pages and your profile, in one zip.

What BioManager sends its makers

Your records never leave your computer or your lab's server. BioManager is free, and a few anonymous counts are how its makers can show that labs rely on it and see which parts to improve first. Each installation sends one short message a day, with counts only:

  • the version, whether it is the desktop app or a server, the operating system and the kind of database;
  • how many members, and how many changed something this week, as ranges (1, 2–5, 6–15, 16–50, 51+);
  • which built-in functions are on, and how many databases of each kind;
  • a random id for the installation, so a lab counts once.

Never names, email addresses, anything anyone wrote, what your databases are called, or the computer's name or address. It goes to PostHog, which is asked not to work out where it came from. Admins see exactly what is sent on the Usage report (Help → Send feedback → Usage report). The counts are on unless an admin switches off Share anonymous counts with BioManager's makers in Settings → General (or Switch off on the Usage report). On a server, BIOMANAGER_TELEMETRY=0 (or DO_NOT_TRACK=1) in .env turns it off for good.

Apart from these counts, the only words of yours that reach BioManager's makers are a feedback note you send them yourself, with Also send this to BioManager's makers ticked (Feedback): that note, its page and the version, and a way to reach you if you gave one.

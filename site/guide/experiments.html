Your databases

Experiments, for every animal

Treatment groups, doses from body weight, readouts and the stats, for every animal.

What this does for your week

An experiment is where a colony turns into data, and it is where records usually fall apart: doses on a paper sheet, weights in one person's phone, the groups remembered rather than written. This page keeps the regimen, the animals and the readout together, so the export is the experiment.

Before it startsName it, set day 1 and the readout, and pull in the animals — a tank's fish or a cohort of mice — so the groups are written down before the first dose.
Each manipulationRecord manipulation writes what was just done to a whole group — Tamoxifen 20 mg/kg i.p. today — and doses are worked out from each animal's body weight rather than in your head.
At the benchScan the experiment's QR code with a phone for bench mode: one animal at a time, gloves on, recorded where you stand (At the bench).
AfterwardsThe chart shows the readout by group over the days. Export gives an Excel workbook — long for Prism or R, wide for a glance, with every animal's manipulations beside it.

What you stop doing: transcribing a dosing sheet, recalculating mg/kg at the cage, and rebuilding the groups from memory when it is time to analyse.

Mice, zebrafish, flies, worms and your own organisms all have an Experiments tab beside their other tabs (Mice and Cages; Tanks and Fish; the vials or plates). New experiment there can start with a whole cage, tank, rack or housing of them. One page shows each experiment, and All experiments on it goes back to the tab.

MiceMice, or a whole cage. Body weight (or tumour volume, a clinical score, survival). Injections, challenges, treatments, surgery, behaviour, stimulation, sampling, imaging.
ZebrafishFish rows, a whole tank, or a clutch's larvae. Survival (how many of those at the start are alive), standard length or a phenotype. Drug in the water, microinjection, heat shock, injury or fin clip.
Flies and wormsVials or plates, or a whole rack; say how many each started with. They have no body weight: survival, or eclosion and climbing for flies, brood size and paralysis for worms. Drug in the food or on the plate, RNAi feeding, temperature shifts, starvation, infection, flipping to fresh food.
Any other organismAnimals, groups or cohorts, or a whole housing. Body weight, length, survival or a score.

Any experiment can follow a readout of its own (Readout → Something else…): a number for each, or how many of those at the start.

Readouts worked out from what you measure

Some readouts are a sum, not a reading: a tumour's volume from the calipers, fold change from a qPCR run. Choose one under Readout → Worked out by BioManager from what you measure, then type what you measured and let BioManager do the arithmetic, the same way every time. New experiment offers them too, with the Control group for ΔΔCt and fold change.

Tumour volume (L × W² / 2)Length and width in mm, the length the longer side. In mm³.
Tumour volume (π/6 × L × W × H)Three diameters in mm, for a lab that measures the height too. Pick whichever your lab uses.
ΔCtThe Ct of the target gene and of the reference gene: target − reference.
ΔΔCt, Fold change (2^−ΔΔCt)The same two Cts, against the mean ΔCt of the Control group (set beside the readout) on the same day. Enter the control animals' Cts first, or the whole day at once.
% of baselineA value and its baseline, such as today's weight and day 1's.
Absorbance, blank subtractedA well's OD and the blank's.
  • Measure (or Record) and Another day open the day with a box for each thing measured, for every animal; click a value in the sheet to change what it was worked out from. Point at a value to see what was measured, and Export has those beside each value.
  • In Bench mode each animal gets a box for each measurement, and the value it worked out is shown as you go on.
  • A number left out, below zero, a width longer than the length, a Ct of 60 or Undetermined is refused, saying why, rather than saved.

Plates from a plate reader or qPCR machine

An ELISA, a viability assay or a qPCR run gives one number per well, and the work is knowing which well was which animal. Lay the plate out once, and each export goes straight into the readout.

  1. Plate layout, above the sheet, opens the plate: 24, 48, 96 or 384 wells (Wells). Pick an animal on the left, or Blank, or Standard with its concentration, then click a well or drag across several; a row letter or column number takes the whole row or column, and Empty clears. Fill in order puts every animal not yet on the plate into the next empty wells, two or three each, across the rows or down the columns. Several wells for one animal are its replicates.
  2. Give it a name and Save layout. It is kept with the experiment, and the next time the plate opens on the layout you last read onto (Same as last plate); change it, or start a New layout.
  3. Read a plate →, then paste the export or choose its file (CSV, TSV or Excel). BioManager reads a grid with row letters A–H and column numbers 1–12 (several reads in one file, such as two wavelengths, can be chosen between), or a list of wells and values: Well and OD, or Well, Target and Ct (or Cq) from a qPCR run.
  4. Readout says what the plate fills: the experiment's main readout, or another measure of the same animals kept beside it (below). Another measure… asks its name and its Kind: A number, with a unit, such as IL-6 ELISA in pg/mL, or one worked out from two genes' Cts, ΔCt: target − reference, ΔΔCt, against a control group or Fold change (2^−ΔΔCt) (the last two ask for the Control group), so a qPCR run can fill a gene's ΔCt beside a tumour volume. The next plate offers it by name, and asks for the genes again.
  5. Each well's value shows on the plate, before anything is saved. A replicate far from the others (more than half a cycle for a Ct, a fifth of the value otherwise) is ringed in orange; a well past what the instrument reads (an OD over 10, a Ct over 50, OVRFLW) in red, and left out, as is Undetermined. Click any well to leave it out, and again to put it back.
  6. Below the plate, each animal's wells, their mean and spread (CV), and the value it gets. Set the Day, then Save readings.
A readout of your own, or another measure(Readout → Something else… at the top, such as IL-6 in pg/mL, or Another measure… in the plate.) The mean of each animal's wells, less the blank's mean (Take off the blank). With standards of two or more concentrations, Read each value off the standard curve turns each into a concentration, and Curve chooses the curve: Linear, a straight line through the standards; Log–log, a straight line on log axes, for standards over several tenfold dilutions; or 4PL, the four-parameter logistic, the S-shaped curve of an ELISA, for five or more concentrations. BioManager starts with 4PL when there are five or more standards spanning a tenfold dilution, and a straight line otherwise. The curve is drawn beside the plate, with the standards as dots and each animal as a square on it, and its equation and r² below. A value past the highest or lowest standard is marked; one past where a 4PL levels off can't be read at all, and says so (dilute the sample and read it again). A 4PL that doesn't fit the standards is refused, in words, rather than used.
Absorbance, blank subtractedThe mean OD less the blank's, kept with both.
ΔCt, ΔΔCt, fold changeChoose the Target gene and the Reference gene from the run (a housekeeping gene on it, such as Gapdh or Actb, starts as the reference); each animal's mean Ct of each is used, and ΔΔCt and fold change are against the Control group as when you type them.

Saving a day that already has readings replaces them, and says so first. The plate is kept with the experiment: Plates read, under the layout, lists each with its day, layout, what it filled and its file. A saved plate is one step in Batch history: Undo there takes back all its readings and the plate together, and puts back any readings it replaced. Only the experiment's owner (or an admin) lays out and reads plates, as with the rest of the experiment.

Another measure beside the main readout

An experiment has one main readout: the one its plan, Main readout day, tests by day and bench mode are about. Something else measured on the same animals, such as an IL-6 ELISA at the end of a tumour experiment, is another measure, kept beside it rather than in its place. Each gets its own button on the sheet, next to the main readout's: the sheet shows its days (a cell can be typed or corrected there), and the chart below and its tests by day follow the button chosen. Export gives each measure a sheet of its own, and its tests under the main readout's in Tests by day. Changing the main readout keeps the other measures.

A measure worked out from Cts is typed in its cell as the two Cts, the target's then the reference's (24.8 − 18.2); a ΔCt measure also takes the ΔCt itself. Pointing at a cell shows the Cts it was worked out from, and its sheet in Export has them beside each value. ΔΔCt and fold change are against the control group's mean ΔCt on the same day, as for the main readout.

The ••• beside the measure shown opens it: change its name or unit (its readings stay with it), its Kind, or the Control group of a ΔΔCt or fold change, or Delete measure. Another kind works its readings out again: a number keeps each value, ΔCt takes each from its two Cts, and ΔΔCt or fold change needs the two Cts of every reading (a number or a typed ΔCt has none, so it says so and changes nothing). Each change is one step in Batch history. Deleting asks first, saying how many readings go with it; it is one step in Batch history, whose Undo brings back the measure and its readings together.

The page

  • The experiment on top: name, status, start (day 1), end, readout and description, saved as you type.
  • The sheet of its animals switches between Treatment — each manipulation day a column: ✓ and the amount for each animal that got it (click to change) — and the readout, a column a day, typed in place (Another day adds one). Each animal's group is edited here too.
  • Record manipulation records what was just done — the HDM group got Tamoxifen 20 mg/kg i.p. today — or a day of the regimen. For a dose per body weight it works out each animal's amount from its latest weight, and the volume given the solution (10 mg/mL). Tick the animals it was done to and, if you want, make a sample record for each one in Samples, with the animal as its source.
  • Each day recorded keeps the Reagent used and its Lot, and the expiry the lot had. It starts as the item picked in the regimen; when the regimen names only what is given (Tamoxifen), the one item of that name in your inventories is picked, and kept on the regimen for next time (with several, they come first in the list: pick the bottle you used). A new bottle is another item, or type its lot. A lot already past its expiry on the day is recorded, with a warning. Tick Take what they got off the inventory to take the amounts (or volumes) the animals got off the item's quantity, turned into its unit (a Pack size turns mL into ampoules); it is taken once, however often the day is saved, and Undo: not done puts it back. The lot shows on the day in the list and the sheet, in Export, and in the experiment's notebook block.
  • The chart shows the readout by group over the days (the value, or its % of the first; for a count, % of those at the start). Each manipulation day is a dashed line: point at it to see what was done that day, by whom. Stars mark the days the groups differ, and Tests by day below lists each test (Welch's t-test for two groups, one-way ANOVA for more, χ² for counts), each day on its own.

The regimen

Regimen lists what is to be done and on which days: the kind, what, dose, route, solution, group and days (1, 2-5, 2/3/4/5, 1, 8, 15). Day 1 is the start date. A Stimulation (optogenetic, electrical, TMS…) also has its Frequency, Intensity, Pulse width and Duration, as you write them: 20 Hz, 5 mW, 5 ms, 30 min. The days to do are on the calendar, and the experiment's owner gets a notification each morning of what is due (turn it off in Settings → Notifications → Experiments). Nothing is done until someone records it on the page. Save as a regimen… keeps it to plan the next cohort from.

Animals that may need a look

Home's card Animals that may need a look reads a mouse experiment's records too. It says what they show, never a diagnosis:

  • Losing weight: a mouse whose latest weight is the lab's percentage (15% unless an admin sets another in Settings → General → Weight loss to flag) or more below its first weight in an experiment that has started, or below its heaviest of the last 30 days.
  • Not weighed in their experiment: in an active experiment whose readout is body weight, an animal (a mouse, or any other organism weighed in the experiment) not weighed for longer than the longest gap between the regimen's weighing days (a Readout day on days 1, 4, 7: 3 days), or 7 days when it plans none or its last has passed. Animals left out of the analysis aren't counted.
  • Status and experiment disagree: a mouse whose status is experiment while it is in no planned, active or paused experiment, or geno, breeder or transfer while it is in an active one.

Planned experiments, and changes from the plan

An AI assistant can design an experiment with you: the groups, the regimen from your saved regimens, how many animals each group needs, and which animals the lab has for it (AI assistants). It arrives as Planned, and nothing of it is due yet: not on Home, the calendar or in notifications.

  1. Check the groups and their animals, the regimen, the Main readout day and the Planned test (Welch's t-test, one-way ANOVA or χ²). Change anything; while it is planned, nothing asks why.
  2. Press Start. Day 1 is today, unless you set a start date. BioManager keeps a copy of the plan.

From then on, four changes ask for a one-line reason, because each can change what the result means: the main readout or its day; the groups (adding, taking out or moving animals, renaming or merging a group); leaving an animal out of the analysis; and the test. Why the change from the plan? takes the reason, and Save with this reason saves both; Cancel leaves it as it was. Nothing is ever refused, and dates, notes and extra readouts never ask.

  • Leave out… on an animal's row keeps its readout in the sheet but leaves it out of the chart and the tests by day, with the reason; Put back counts it again.
  • Changes from the plan lists each change: what, before → after, the reason, who and when. Below it, Planned → done sets the plan beside what happened.
  • The Excel export gets a Plan and deviations sheet, so a paper's methods can say what was set in advance and what changed.
  • Experiments started before this, and those made without a plan, never ask.

At the bench, and afterwards

  • Bench mode (scan its QR code with a phone): pick what you are doing — the main readout, another measure of the experiment, or a manipulation due — then one animal at a time with big numbers: type each weight, count or value, or give each today's manipulation and tap Given ✓; record it at the end, with the Reagent used and its Lot filled in as on the page. Scan a card reads a cage, tank or vial card with the phone's camera and jumps to the animal in it.
  • Export downloads an Excel workbook: the readout, long (for Prism or R) and wide, each animal's manipulations with the reagent and lot it got, the tests by day, and a sheet for each other measure.
  • Add to notebook makes your notebook page for the experiment, with a live view of its manipulations and readout (/experiment adds one to any page; Freeze a copy keeps it as it is now).

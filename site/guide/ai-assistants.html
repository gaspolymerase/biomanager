AI assistants

AI assistants

Tell an AI assistant what you did, or ask it to plan; it proposes the records, and you approve them in BioManager.

Lab server or desktop

BioManager has no AI inside it. If you already use an assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, Codex, Cursor, Cherry Studio or another app that speaks MCP), you can connect it to your lab. Tell it what you did, in your own words or with a photo of your notebook (litter in 102 yesterday, 7 pups; moved the two old breeders from 88 to 91; injected day 3 of the TMX cohort this morning), and it turns that into records: as one proposal, which you approve in BioManager. It never changes anything itself.

Connect an assistant

Open Help → Connect an AI assistant (also under Settings → AI assistant & tokens). It shows the ways in for your BioManager, with its address filled in. Step by step for each app, with what to check first: Connect an AI assistant.

Add the BioManager plugin to your assistant too, once (Claude, Codex, VS Code, Qwen Code; from gaspolymerase/biomanager; Kimi Code from its own download). It teaches the assistant how to work with BioManager, so you never have to explain it: say what you did, or just I want to change something in BioManager, and it shows you what's due today and asks what you did.

What to say

Talk to it as you would to a colleague who keeps the records. The plugin also gives your assistant these commands (in Claude, type /):

Log today's workWeaned 110, weighed 6 and 7, moved 30 and 31 to 106. It looks up each animal, cage and experiment, asks when something could be more than one record, and sends one proposal.
Log from a photoA photo of your notebook, the whiteboard or cage cards. It reads the numbers, asks about any it can't read, and proposes.
Process my capturesWhat you wrote down or photographed at the bench (below) becomes records.
Plan tomorrowWhat's due, grouped by room and time, overdue first.
Review the lab this weekThe week's checks (below), and one proposal for the clean-up.
Design an experimentFrom a question to a planned experiment (below).
Write the methodsA methods paragraph and the ARRIVE checklist for an experiment, from its records (below).
Animal-use reportThe animals used under each protocol over the year, for the annual review (below).
Write a handoverEverything someone leaving looks after, and what is due on it (below).

You can also just ask: Which lot of tamoxifen did cohort 3 get?, Where's the last tube of pAAV-hSyn-GCaMP8m?, Who changed cage 88's purpose? It searches your records and notebook, reads a record's history and a plasmid's or a mouse's family tree, and answers with links. It reads only what you can read. Upload a clone's sequencing read (.ab1) as a capture and ask does clone 3 match plasmid 14?: BioManager checks it against the map, as Verify sequencing does, and the assistant tells you each difference and what it does to the protein.

Captures: write it down now, record it later

At the rack you rarely have time for a conversation. Capture keeps what you'd tell the assistant until you have:

  • On a phone, open Captures in your account menu and press New capture, or press Capture in an experiment's Bench mode. Type, or use the keyboard's microphone to dictate; add a photo with Take photo, or a file (a balance's or plate reader's CSV, or a sequencing read) with Choose file; Scan a card attaches the cage, tank or vial it's about. Save.
  • Later, at your desk, tell the assistant process my captures. It reads each, photos and files included, and sends one proposal; each change says which capture it came from.
  • Captures lists yours, waiting ones first. Approving a proposal marks the captures it used as used, with the proposal; Dismiss sets one aside (Keep it waiting brings it back). Only you see your captures.

What it can propose

  • In the mouse colony, zebrafish, fly and worm databases and any other organism's: the everyday records. New animals, cages, tanks and vials; litters (with how many pups were born), weaning, moves and deaths; crosses, clutches and flips; weights and readings; scheduled jobs done.
  • In your inventories: a new item, a change, an amount used (the item turns low at its Low at; say it in another unit of weight or volume, 40 mg of an item kept in g, and BioManager converts it; 2 mL of one kept in ampoules once it has a Pack size), an order received (and added to stock under the name, amount and unit your stock keeps), a sample taken from an animal; and an inventory renumbered, so each # is the number in its name (Renumber, below).
  • In plasmids: a new plasmid (with its sequence pasted as GenBank or FASTA), a change, a glycerol stock, a primer or a primer pair.
  • In an experiment: a step done (with the reagent and lot used: the step's own, unless you name another bottle or lot; an unknown lot is refused, and with several bottles of the same name it asks which), a readout, animals added, its status, a note; and a new, planned experiment (below).
  • In the notebook: a line in today's daily log; a new page (marked Drafted by the assistant, never signed); a section added to a page, unless someone has it open. To fix a page it made, or one of yours, it changes that page rather than making another: its title, kind, tags, or its text, all of it or a few parts (steps rewritten as a checklist, so Run can tick them off). You see each change before and after; the text as it was is kept in the page's History, and a signed page, or one someone has open, is left alone. It can also make a folder in Protocols and file protocols in it.
  • In the calendar: an event, or an instrument booking.
  • It can't delete anything, sign a page, start an experiment, or change who may see what.

Planning: breeding, cohorts, experiments

For anything with a right answer, the assistant asks BioManager, which works it out from your lab's own records, and tells you the assumptions:

  • Which animals are free: by genotype, sex and age on a date, leaving out those in experiments and breeding cages.
  • A breeding plan: I need 12 Cre⁺ fl/fl males at 8 weeks by March 1. The odds for each mating, from the breeders you have; how many matings to set up, and by when, counting every litter a mating has time for and the pairs already in Breeding cages; your line's own litter sizes; up to five genes. It says when two of the crosses it lists would need the same breeders, and which breeders are in an experiment. A strain (C57BL/6J, BALB/c) isn't an allele: wanting it means both parents of that strain.
  • Animals per group: from the difference you want to see and the spread in your own past readouts (the same numbers G*Power gives).
  • Random groups: balanced for sex and litter, with weights even across groups; the seed is kept, in a new experiment's description.
  • When stock runs out: from how fast each item has been used.
  • Readouts from raw numbers: a caliper sheet, a qPCR export or a plate-reader file becomes tumour volumes, ΔΔCt and fold change, or blank-subtracted ODs, worked out by BioManager and shown to you before it proposes them (Experiments).
  • A plate on its layout: give it the plate reader's or qPCR machine's export (or a capture of its file), and it is read onto the experiment's plate layout: each animal's wells averaged, the replicates far from the others and the wells past what the instrument reads named, and the readings proposed for you to approve (Experiments).

Designing an experiment. Tell it the question (does deleting Il33 in airway epithelium reduce inflammation in our HDM model? Start in about six weeks). It proposes the groups and controls, the main readout, how many animals and which, the regimen from your saved regimens, and the test, and says what it assumed. Doses for something the lab hasn't used come with a source, and checking the design against your animal protocol is yours. Approved, it is a planned experiment: nothing is due until you press Start, and after that, changes to the plan ask why (Experiments).

A weekly review

Review the lab this week runs BioManager's checks: overdue jobs, the animals that may need a look (losing weight past the lab's threshold, notes with a welfare word, not weighed in an experiment, status and experiment disagreeing: the same as Home's card, Home), empty cages still in a rack, breeders past 30 weeks, notebook pages left unsigned, reagents expired, expiring or low, and proposals still waiting. It sums them up by how much they matter and offers one proposal for what can be cleaned up (a mouse in an active experiment whose status says geno gets its status set to experiment). For an animal it says what the records show, with the numbers and the note's own line, never a diagnosis. An admin or lab manager can have their assistant run it every Monday (in Claude, as a scheduled task).

Write-ups

Write the methods for the TMX cohort gives a methods paragraph built only from the records: strains, sex, age and numbers per group, each manipulation with its dose, route, days and lot (and any lot used after it expired), the readout and the tests. With it comes the ARRIVE 2.0 Essential 10, each item with what the records say and what's missing, and the experiment's changes from its plan. It can put the draft in a new notebook page, marked as drafted by the assistant.

The animal-use report. Draft our animal-use report for protocol 2024-0012 gives, for the last twelve months (or the dates you say), a table per protocol of the mice, zebrafish and your own organisms by strain: how many were born, added to the records, used in experiments, died or were euthanized (with the reasons their notes give) and kept for breeding. BioManager does the counting, and the report says what each number is based on and what the records can't say, such as animals with no protocol. A mouse's protocol is the colony's own protocol column (add one under Configure → Your own columns, called IACUC # or Protocol), else, for a pup, its mother's or father's; pups a litter counted that have no record of their own are under their parents' protocol too. A fish's is its line's IACUC #; another organism's is its own Protocol, else its housing's, else its line's. It is a draft to check against your institution's form.

A handover. Write a handover for me before you leave (an admin can ask for anyone's) lists everything in your name: cages and mice by rack and position, breeding cages and what's due on them, tanks, vials, inventory items and glycerol stocks with where each is, plasmids, experiments and their next steps, racks and boxes you made, notebook pages not signed and proposals waiting, each with its link, then everything due in the next 30 days. Ask it to, and it proposes giving the records to whoever takes them over (as Set field → Owner would); someone else's notebook pages stay theirs, counted but not named.

Approving

A proposal tells you (the bell) and waits under Proposed changes in your account menu.

  • Each proposal is one summary in plain words, with any warnings at the top (a litter date that would be replaced, pups too young to wean, a cage that would stop being shared with the lab, an item of that name already in stock), then one line for each change, its database's icon beside it. On the right of a line is what it came from: You said (point at it to read your words), or Photo and the line of the page it read. Show each change lists every record, before → after, with your words for each change.
  • Approve makes every change at once; Discard makes none. If something is wrong, Send back with a line saying what: the assistant reads it and proposes again, corrected, and the new proposal takes the old one's place.
  • An approved proposal is one batch in Batch history, marked with the assistant's name, so Undo takes it back whole (a line added to the notebook stays).
  • If a record it touches was changed after the proposal was made, or a change no longer goes through, nothing is applied and the page says which record and why.
  • Only you can approve your proposals, and only signed in to BioManager: no token can. A proposal you leave waits two weeks, then expires.

Troubleshooting

The assistant can't reach BioManager, or doesn't seem to know it

See Connect an AI assistant.

Approving says a record changed after the proposal was made

Someone (or you) changed it meanwhile, so the proposal may be out of date. Discard it and ask the assistant to propose again.

It worked out a number itself

Ask it to use BioManager's tool for it (breeding plan, animals per group, groups), and to say the assumptions. With the plugin it does so on its own.

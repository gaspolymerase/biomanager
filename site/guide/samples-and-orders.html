Samples, orders, reagents, antibodies, viruses

Samples, orders, reagents, chemicals, antibodies, viruses, primers, glycerol stocks and cell lines.

What this does for your week

Buying something and finding it again are the same chain, and it usually breaks where one person orders and another unpacks the box. BioManager keeps that chain in one place, so the shelf matches the record.

Someone needs somethingNew order will not go out without item, vendor, catalogue number and quantity, which is what makes a reorder possible months later.
While it is comingRequested → Ordered → Received, one tap each, on a table or a board you drag cards across. Everyone can see what is on its way instead of asking.
The box arrivesMark it received and BioManager offers to put it on the shelf — Receive an order into stock makes the reagent, antibody or virus record with the vendor, catalogue number and lot already filled in.
Months laterExpiring soon and Expired chips, and both on Home. On anything running low, Order again opens a new order already filled in from the old one.

What you stop doing: ordering a second one because nobody knew the first had arrived, retyping a catalogue number off a box, and finding the antibody expired the morning you needed it.

One engine, ten starting points, each with its own columns and statuses. Viruses, Primers and Glycerol stocks each name the plasmid they came from, so they sit with Plasmids as tabs of one area, Molecular biology in the sidebar, rather than on their own:

SamplesTissue and material, linked to the animal it came from — pick the colony and type its ID in the Source cell, or tick a whole harvest and set it on all of them at once. Beside it, Custom tag shows that mouse's tag, straight from the colony, so re-tagging a mouse there updates every sample of it at once. If you hide Custom tag under Columns on the Mice tab, it goes from Samples too. Stored at RT, 4 °C, −20 °C, −80 °C or LN₂ in a freezer box position, with what was measured as numbers: Conc. and its unit, 260/280, 260/230, Volume (µL). Available, in use, used up, discarded.
OrdersWhat the lab has asked for: vendor, catalogue number, quantity, price, grant account. Requested, ordered, received, cancelled — on a table or a Board you drag cards across.
ReagentsQuantity, concentration, CAS number, storage temperature, hazard, lot and expiry. In stock, low, empty, discarded.
ChemicalsThe lab's chemical list, as you may keep it in Excel now: name, Abbreviation, CAS, MW (g/mol), Purity (%), and in the record Formula and Density (g/mL); with quantity, lot, expiry, hazard and where each bottle is. A Formulation in a notebook page picks chemicals from here by name, abbreviation or CAS number and works out the moles from what you weigh, and the calculators in Utilities know each molecular weight. In stock, low, empty, discarded.
AntibodiesHost, clonality, clone, conjugate, reactivity, applications, dilution, isotype, RRID, and where each vial is.
VirusesAAV, lentivirus, rabies and other vectors: serotype, the plasmid it was made from (Made from), promoter, payload, titer, biosafety level, the date made, and each aliquot's place in a −80 °C box. The DNA icon beside the plasmid opens its sequence and map to read (editing stays on the plasmid's own page). In stock, low, used up, discarded.
Primers & oligosSequence (5′→3′), direction, target, its pair, stock µM and modification. Length, GC % and Tm are worked out from the sequence (nearest neighbour, 50 mM Na⁺, 250 nM). Add primer pair makes name-F and name-R at once, each naming the other, side by side in a box. A primer drawn on a plasmid's map comes here by itself, with the plasmid in its Plasmid column. Tick primers, then Copy for ordering or Export for ordering for the supplier. To order, ordered, in stock, low, used up, discarded.
Glycerol stocksBacteria carrying a plasmid, frozen in glycerol: the plasmid (Plasmid), strain, colony, resistance, how it was checked (digest, colony PCR, Sanger, whole plasmid), the date frozen, and its place in a −80 °C box. The plasmid's Storage tab lists its glycerol stocks and the box each is in. In stock, low, used up, discarded.
Cell linesA frozen vial of each line or clone: species, parent, passage, freeze date, cells per vial, medium, selection, mycoplasma result and date, and its place in the LN₂ boxes. In stock, thawed, used up, discarded.
Custom listWhatever you define.

Ordering, start to shelf

  1. New order. Item, vendor, catalogue number and quantity are required (starred), so no order goes out half-filled. Type an item or catalogue number the lab has used before and pick it: the vendor, price and grant fill in from last time. The lab's admins are told of each new request.
  2. Follow it. The status chips — Requested, Ordered, Received, Cancelled — show each stage in one tap; the person who requested it is notified as it moves.
  3. It arrives. Mark it received and BioManager asks Add it to stock?: a new reagent, chemical, antibody or virus with its name, vendor, catalogue number, lot, quantity and expiry already filled in, opened for you to add where it's kept. (Later, the To stock button on the order does the same.) From then on the order shows Reagents #8 and the reagent shows the order it came from; each opens the other.
  4. Running low? On any reagent, chemical, antibody or virus, the cart button, Order again, opens a new order with its details and the quantity, price and grant of the last time it was ordered. While it is already on order the button says On order, and the new order opens with a note naming the open one and who asked for it.

Every inventory

  • Mine and Lab common keep your own stock apart from the lab's shared shelf.
  • Expiring soon (30 days) and Expired chips; expired and low stock also show on Home.
  • Box grid places items in freezer boxes; the same boxes as everywhere else. A box's Stored at (−80 °C, LN₂…) goes to what is put in it, and moving the box moves theirs. In a box with no position given, an item takes the next free one. Marking a tube used up, empty or discarded frees its position; its location note keeps where it was. New box has How many, for a whole tower at once.
  • Used in notebook pages, at the bottom of a record's dialog, lists the pages that link it (@antibodies 5; see Notebook).
  • Configure (its creator, or an admin): the name, what it tracks, the choices and statuses (renaming one relabels its items), your own columns, and which fields are needed for a new item.
  • A plasmid column (the Viruses' Made from, or one you add in Configure as A plasmid): type the plasmid's number or name, or pick it from the list, and the cell opens that plasmid. The plasmid's Storage tab lists everything made from it, under Made from this plasmid.

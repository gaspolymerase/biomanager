## BioManager 1.1.0

### New

- **AI assistants.** Tell an assistant you already use (Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, Cherry Studio) what you did, in your own words or with a photo of your notebook, and it proposes the records for you to approve. BioManager has no AI inside it.
  - The assistant reads what you can read, as you, and **proposes**; it never changes anything itself. A proposal waits for you under **Proposed changes** in your account menu, with a notification, as one plain summary. **Approve** makes every change at once, as one batch that **Batch history** can undo; **Discard** makes none.
  - It can propose the everyday records: in the mouse colony, zebrafish, fly and worm databases and any other organism's (new animals and cages, litters, weaning, moves, deaths, weights, crosses, jobs done), in an experiment (a step done, a readout, animals added) and a line in today's notebook log. It can't delete anything, sign a page or change who sees what.
  - **Help → Connect an AI assistant** sets it up. For Claude Code, Cursor or Cherry Studio on this computer or the lab network, copy the message there into your assistant and it sets itself up; you then sign in in your browser and press **Allow**, with no token to copy.
  - On a lab server that is on the internet, **claude.ai, the Claude phone apps and ChatGPT** add BioManager as a custom connector and sign in with a one-time connection code from the same page.
  - The connection is listed under **Settings → API tokens**; revoke it there to cut the assistant off.
- **Plasmids:**
  - **Versions**, below the map: every earlier sequence (replaced, cleared or edited), with **Restore**. **Download** gives the sequence as GenBank, with every feature, or FASTA.
  - **Made from**, on Properties: the backbone, insert or template a plasmid came from, in the lab or from outside it (such as Addgene #11150), and how it was made. The page shows **Made from** and **Used to make** above the map, and a **Family tree**.
  - **Feature library** (at the top of Plasmids): the lab's own elements by sequence. **Add to library** above a well-annotated map, or **Read elements from files** on the library page, fills it; an admin can fetch a common-features pack of about 1,900 elements when the lab asks for it. **Detect features** then marks them on any plasmid.
  - **Assemble a plasmid** builds the next plasmid from the ones you have: digest and ligate, Gibson / NEBuilder HiFi (it designs the primers) or Golden Gate. You see the product's map and every junction's bases before anything is made, and the new plasmid knows what it was made from.
  - **Files**, on Storage: the sequencing reads that confirmed a plasmid, gel photos, the vendor's datasheet.
  - A primer drawn on a map is kept in **Primers**, marked to order, and **Copy for ordering** gives the ticked ones ready for a supplier's form.
  - **Glycerol stocks**: a new database for the bacteria carrying each plasmid, with each tube's place in a −80 °C box. Lab setup offers it.
  - On a lab server, whoever looks after it can install pLannotate; **Annotate with pLannotate** then appears above the map.
- **The rest of BioManager is in Chinese (中文)**: the other databases, experiments, the notebook, Lab setup, the admin pages and the messages after you save or change something. What your lab typed always stays as typed.

### What works differently

- **Primers, Glycerol stocks and Viruses are tabs at the top of Plasmids**, in the order the work runs, instead of separate entries in the sidebar. Each is the same database at the same address.
- A sequence file keeps what it says: a GenBank or SnapGene file names the plasmid and keeps every annotation note, a circular plasmid stays circular, and a file of several sequences keeps the first and says so.

### Updating

- **Desktop app**: *Install and Restart* when it offers 1.1.0, or download it below. Your data is kept outside the app, and a copy is taken before the database is upgraded.
- **Lab server**:
  1. Take a fresh backup (`docker compose exec backup backup.sh`).
  2. Unpack the new `biomanager-server.tar.gz` over the old folder. Your `.env`, backups and certificates are kept.
  3. Run `host/load-image.sh`, then `docker compose up -d --build`.

  The database upgrades itself on start, adding the tables for proposals, assistants' sign-ins and the plasmids' versions, parents, feature library and files; every earlier release's database was checked to upgrade with nothing lost. Details are under *Updating the app* in the runbook (`deploy/RUNBOOK.md` in the bundle).
  - For claude.ai, the Claude apps or ChatGPT, the server must be on the internet (*Letting a guest in from the internet* in the runbook). Claude Code, Cursor and the others need nothing more.
